# The business, A to Z: market data, DataBP and its reconciliation app

| | |
|---|---|
| **What this is** | The summary of the business the demo serves, from the exchange to our app, as shown above the diagram on the business page's first view |
| **Published at** | The DataBP demo site, business.html, first view: https://integrationals.github.io/databp-demo/business.html |
| **Terms** | Each term's meaning and source is in the course log, knowledge 111 item 798, entries 312 to 327 |

Exchanges sell the market data their trading produces, through vendors, to subscribers who use it to trade and to protect their money. Four documents describe each subscriber's use; DataBP builds software for the sellers, and our app checks that the four agree.

| # | Who or what | In short |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | The exchange | Produces market data and licenses it to vendors |
| 2 | The vendor | Distributes the data, permits subscribers, reports their use |
| 3 | The subscriber | Pays to trade, value holdings, manage risk, prove best execution |
| 4 | The four documents | License, entitlement, usage report, invoice |
| 5 | DataBP and our app | Software for the sellers; the app checks the four agree |

1. An exchange is a market where shares, bonds, options and futures trade, and it sells the data its trading produces. In its market data agreement it grants a license to a data recipient: limited, non-exclusive, non-transferable and revocable, for the agreement's term, to use the data and to distribute it to subscribers who sign a subscriber agreement. Sources: course log entries 317 and 318; MEMX Market Data Agreement, section 4.
2. Vendors include market data vendors, broker-dealers and private network providers. A vendor permits each subscriber to receive the data through an entitlement control the exchange approves, and each month submits a usage report to the exchange counting the units each subscriber used, sometimes from counts the subscriber supplies. Sources: an NYSE filing of 2010-05-12; Euronext Reporting Guidelines v1.6, sections 2.3 and 2.4 and appendix A2.2.
3. Subscribers pay because the data feeds decisions that make or protect money: people trading from screens (display use, charged per screen); machines trading by algorithm, order routing or market making (non-display use, charged apart); pricing and valuing what they hold; watching risk during the day; and, for brokers, best execution, the duty to obtain the best terms for customers' orders, measured against the NBBO, the national best bid and national best offer: for a stock, the highest priced bid and the lowest priced offer across the exchanges quoting it, and the one price the business's operational side is checked against. Sources: EY, on managing market data costs; Regulation NMS, 17 CFR 242.600, and Nasdaq's summary of the SEC's proposed changes to it; Nasdaq, filing SR-NASDAQ-2024-016; Markets Media, "Market data fees: an overview"; FINRA, Regulatory Notice 26-15; SIFMA, comment letter of 2026-08-17.
4. Each subscriber's use is described by four documents: the license, what was granted; the entitlement, what is permitted; the usage report, what was used, in units of count; and the invoice, what was billed, by the exchange or by the vendor passing the fees on. They should agree. Sources: course log entries 312 to 319; Databento's market data licensing FAQ, on who bills.
5. DataBP builds software for the sellers: exchanges, trading platforms, index and data providers, data vendors and fintechs. It turns sales opportunities in Salesforce into licensing agreements and offers its Licensing Ledger, which since 2026-10-08 the London Stock Exchange's customers use to check their entitlements against their invoices: entitlement and billing reconciliation. Our app, under the same name, does it for one seller: it compares the four documents for each of its subscribers, shows each disagreement with its dollar amount, and what changed since the last month-end close. Sources: DataBP's site; Kaiko, on its partnership with DataBP; the London Stock Exchange, 2026-10-08; the design document, knowledge 111 item 806.
