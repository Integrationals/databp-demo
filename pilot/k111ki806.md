## 1. Purpose

This design decides the end product of the DataBP demo and works backwards from it to the data it needs: entitlement and billing reconciliation, a Hex app named for its question with the London Stock Exchange's term for the same check on the customer's side (course log, knowledge 111 item 798, entry 321), for one of DataBP's clients, a data provider, that answers whether the provider's records about each subscriber agree, and what changed since the last month-end close. The problem it serves is DataBP's own headline: "Less than half of contracted market data revenue is ever realized", drained by leakage, errors and audits that recover only a fraction of what they cost (databp.com, "The Problem" and "Resolve Revenue Leaks", read 2026-11-19; DataBP calls the figure an estimate and names no source). Ray, 2026-11-19: "the whole point here ... we must hone in on it." Each of the app's five checks is aimed at one of those causes (D-10, R-5). It is written for Ray, who decides, and for the session that builds it; its audience in the demo is Bin Luo, DataBP's CTO and the hiring manager.

## 3. Concept

The product is a reconciliation with memory. A data provider, such as an exchange, licenses its data to subscribers, firms that use the data and pay for it. Four records describe each subscriber: the license (the contract: what it bought and how many units), the entitlement (what the platform lets it receive), the usage report (what it reports it used each month) and the invoice (what it was billed). They should agree. The app names each place they do not, with its dollar amount, and shows what changed since the last month-end close, which only kept history can show.

## 6. Domain specification

### 6.1 source-to-target map

Two hops, as decision D-18 sets: bronze to silver here, and silver to gold, the mart of decision D-21, below it once agreed. Bronze is the connector's copy of each source table in `DATABP_LAB`, read through one staging view per table that renames, casts and carries the deleted flag (D-20). Silver is seven tables in schema `SILVER`, one per source document, each named for the thing it holds (Object Naming, knowledge 12 item 147, rules 1 to 3), each keyed by an md5 hash of its business key qualified by its issuer's namespace (D-19).

The keys, defined once and used in the map by name. A business key is the issuer's code, the table's code and the key's parts, joined by a vertical bar; its hash is md5 of that text.

| Key | Business key | Issuer |
|---|---|---|
| client_hk | DATABP, CLIENT, client_id | The DataBP platform |
| subscriber_hk | DATABP, CUSTOMER, tenant_id, customer_id | The DataBP platform |
| product_hk | DATABP, PRODUCT, tenant_id, product_code | The DataBP platform |
| license_line_hk | SFDC, OPPORTUNITYLINEITEM, Id | Salesforce |
| entitlement_hk | DATABP, ENTITLEMENT, tenant_id, entitlement_id | The DataBP platform |
| usage_report_hk | USAGE, USAGE_DECLARATION, tenant_id, declaration_id | Usage reporting |
| invoice_hk | DATABP, INVOICE, tenant_id, invoice_id | The DataBP platform |
| invoice_item_hk | DATABP, INVOICE_ITEM, tenant_id, invoice_id, item_id | The DataBP platform |

Salesforce's Account.Tenant__c and Subscriber_Id__c, and the usage report's tenant_id and customer_id, are the platform's identifiers, so their subscriber_hk is built in the platform's namespace and equals the platform's own; product_hk likewise from Product2.Tenant__c and ProductCode, and from the usage report's product_code. An entitlement or invoice item names its product by product_id, so its product_hk is built from the product's code, looked up through product. Each silver row also carries change_hash, md5 of its non-key columns, and landed_at, the connector's insert time (its _SNOWFLAKE_INSERTED_AT), as the reference platform's integrators carry their load time; a deleted row at the source is not in silver.

### 6.5 Worked examples

Acme Capital subscribes to a data provider's equities feed. Its license says 10 units. The platform entitles it to 12. Its usage report for October says 14. Its October invoice bills 10. The app shows three facts for Acme Capital: usage beyond the license by 4 units, entitlement beyond the license by 2, and billing that matches the license but not the usage. At the feed's unit price of 50 dollars, 4 units beyond license is 200 dollars unbilled for October.

Acme Capital also subscribes to the provider's options feed. Its license says 8 units and the platform entitles it to 8. Its usage report for October says 5, and its October invoice bills 5. The app shows one fact for this product: entitlement beyond the usage report by 3 units, the under-reporting check of D-10. At the feed's unit price of 40 dollars, that is 120 dollars at risk for October. Across its two products Acme Capital shows three of the five checks; the planted one case carries all five (design sketch, knowledge 111 item 805, section 8.5).
